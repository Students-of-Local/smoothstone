# stone timelapse --fail

Shows how this print would fail, as a video, from the same camera as stone timelapse. It is a
physics model, not a recording. It does not print.

## When to run it
- The person asks "what happens without supports?", "what if it comes loose?", "show me it
  failing", or wants to see why stone says supports are needed.
- After stone build, or on any STL or sliced G-code.

## Rules
- Run: stone timelapse --fail (the built part) or stone timelapse <file.stl> --fail.
  That is auto: it shows the first failure the model predicts for this print.
- "No failure predicted; nothing to show." means the model says it prints. Tell them that. Make a
  video only if they still want one: --fail detach shows a loose part anyway.
- To see it without supports: add --supports off. --fail supports, droop and bridge draw the part
  without supports by themselves (not with --gcode).
- Named modes, only when asked: --fail supports, --fail detach, --fail droop, --fail bridge.
  "What-if" in the output means the model does not predict it for this print: it holds, or it has
  supports. Say so.
- "Nothing here fails by bridge" (or another mode): tell them that. There is no video.
- "PLA is assumed": ask what plastic it is, then add --material (PETG, ABS, ASA, PC, PA for nylon, TPU,
  PLA-CF or PETG-CF). "No numbers for HIPS, so this is PLA's": tell them the video is
  that plastic's (the line names it), not theirs.
- "A corner lifting is not judged" (PC, nylon, TPU): nobody has published how hard these hold a plate.
  Say the model cannot tell whether a corner lifts; it can still show one, with --fail detach.
- --plate dirty: a plate that needs cleaning (holds about 5% as well). Use it when they ask about
  a dirty plate or a part that came loose.
- Always tell the person:
  1. It is a model, not a recording.
  2. What fails and when, from the "Predicted:" or "What-if" line.
  3. The "Main assumption:" line, in plain words.
- The video is fail.mp4, in build/ or in <file name>-timelapse/. Look at fail_sheet.png before you
  describe it: the frames before the failure, if any (at most 2), are the normal print. The rest are
  the failure, closer in and without the toolhead, which would hide it.
- The words: supports = lines print into air and fall (spaghetti). droop = an edge sags down.
  bridge = a long bridge sags. detach = the part comes loose and slides. topple = it tips over.
- Then run the NEXT line it printed.

## Example
Request: What happens if I print the T without supports?
stone timelapse t.stl --supports off --fail ->
  This is a model, not a recording: failsim's physics on the toolpath generated by stone, ...
  Material: PLA is assumed; say --material PETG, ABS, ASA, PC, PA, TPU, PLA-CF or PETG-CF.
  This print's own settings: inner wall 300 mm/s, line 0.42 mm.
  The K2 profile's, not the print's own: bed, nozzle, fan, infill, chamber (fan full on).
  Predicted: supports at layer 21 (z 4.2 mm, 20 s in): lines with nothing under them print into
  air and fall (spaghetti).
  Why: ledge 12.0 mm past the layer below droops 11.654 mm.
  Main assumption: Droop of one layer fails the ledge; half a layer is the smallest droop that
  shows. (failsim.droop_rule)
  Video: .../t.stl-timelapse/fail.mp4 (32 frames, 4.0 s; from layer 21 on: the model, closer in,
  only the nozzle drawn)
  Strand: 0.48 mm thick, it leaves the nozzle at 60.0 mm/s while the nozzle moves at 127.9. Pulling
  it that fast takes 5.13 mN; 100 mm of it on the plate holds 0.11 mN, so the hotend drags it along
  until something holds it.
  Air and heat: it falls at 2.3 m/s at most, and sets in 8.17 s (1.16 s in the fan's air). The
  nozzle's tip carries 8.0 mm3.
  Sheet: .../t.stl-timelapse/fail_sheet.png
  Data: .../t.stl-timelapse/fail.json (31 s)
  NEXT: ask the person: the model says this print fails (supports); print it with supports, or the
  part turned over?
Reply: Here is a model of it (fail.mp4), not a real recording. Without supports, the arms of the
T start at layer 21 (4.2 mm up) with nothing under them. Those lines fall to the bed, and the
hotend drags them along into spaghetti, because the plate holds them less than it takes to pull
them thin. Shall I print it with supports on, or turned upside down? (I took it as PLA.)

Request: My plate was dirty. Show me the plate coming loose.
stone timelapse plate.stl --fail --plate dirty ->
  Predicted: detach at layer 1 (z 0.2 mm, 1 min 29 s in): the part comes loose from the plate;
  later layers print into air.
  Why: edge pull 2.69 MPa against a plate bond of 1.52 MPa (margin 0.56).
  Main assumption: A contaminated plate keeps the same fraction of its hold as PP keeps of
  PLA's on a clean plate. (failsim.contamination)
  Limit: 294 s of printing into air is not drawn: the video follows 60 s of it, the start of each
  layer.
  NEXT: ask the person: the model says this print fails (detach); print it with a clean plate and a
  brim?
Reply: In the model, a dirty plate lets go after the first layer. The edges pull harder (2.7 MPa)
than the plate holds (1.5 MPa). Then later layers print into air and a blob grows on the nozzle.
It draws 60 s of that mess; a real one would be bigger. Shall we clean the plate with soap and water
and add a brim? (I took it as PLA.)
