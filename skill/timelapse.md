# stone timelapse

Shows, before printing, what the printer's own timelapse of this print will look like: the same
layers, in the same order, at the same times, from the printer's camera. It does not print.

## When to run it
- The person asks "what will this look like printing?", "how long will it take?", or wants to
  see the print grow before they start it.
- After stone build, or on any STL or sliced G-code.

## Rules
- Run: stone timelapse (the built part), stone timelapse <file.stl>, or
  stone timelapse --gcode <file.gcode> when they have sliced it. G-code is the most exact.
- Read the "Toolpaths:" line. "from the G-code" is the slicer's own plan. "generated by stone"
  is stone's guess at it: tell the person the times are close, not exact.
- Tell them the print time from the "Print time:" line.
- The "Video:" and "Sheet:" lines name the files. The sheet shows 9 frames: look at it before you
  describe the print.
- All of it is drawn in one color. "no filament named" means the color is only a guess.
- Options, only when asked:
  - --night: the black and white night camera look.
  - --mode realtime: frames at even steps of time, so slow layers last longer.
  - --seconds N: the video's length.
  - --supports on or off: force supports. The default follows stone supports.
- Then run the NEXT line it printed.

## Fit the camera (once per printer)
The camera pose is a guess until it is fit to a real snapshot from the printer's camera.
1. Set the printer first: stone printer set <id>.
2. Get a snapshot (jpg) that shows all 4 corners of the bed, with the nozzle down at the bed (Z 0) or,
   on a printer whose bed slides, the bed in the middle. Otherwise note that Z (or Y) for --snap-at.
3. Find the bed's 4 corners in pixels: front left, front right, back right, back left.
4. stone timelapse --camera-from snap.jpg --bed-corners "x1,y1 x2,y2 x3,y3 x4,y4" --save-camera
5. A FIX line means a corner is off or the order is wrong: look again. A good fit can still start at the
   wrong corner of a square bed, so NEXT asks the person if the camera sits where the fit says. Ask it.
--save-camera keeps the pose for every job on that printer. Without it, only this job uses it.

## Example
Request: Show me how the hook prints standing up.
stone timelapse hook_up.stl ->
  Toolpaths: generated by stone, no G-code: 2 walls, 15% grid infill, 3 bottom and 5 top solid
  layers, supports on (stone supports says needed), OrcaSlicer's K2 speeds.
  Printer: Creality K2 (none set; stone printer set <id>); camera pose from the profile, an assumption
  Print time: 20 min 57 s, 385 layers, red filament.
  Video: .../hook_up.stl-timelapse/timelapse.mp4 (385 frames, 12.8 s, per-layer)
  Sheet: .../hook_up.stl-timelapse/timelapse_sheet.png (9 frames)
  Data: .../hook_up.stl-timelapse/timelapse.json (rendered in 158 s, 172 s in all)
  NEXT: none, the timelapse is made
Reply: Here is the timelapse (timelapse.mp4). It prints in about 21 minutes, 385 layers, with
supports under the round bottom of the hook for the first 3 mm. These are stone's own toolpaths;
slice it for an exact time.
